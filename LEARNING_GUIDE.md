## Introduction

By the end of Primary 6 in Singapore, students are expected to be proficient in Malay across various skills – reading, writing, speaking, and listening. The PSLE Malay exam covers: composition writing, language use (grammar and vocabulary), reading comprehension (kefahaman), oral communication, and listening comprehension.

This guide compiles key knowledge and notes to help a Primary student (especially a non-native speaker) master the Malay language by Secondary 1, with extra content to stretch the learner.

## Vocabulary Building and Expressions (Perbendaharaan Kata)

### Expanding Vocabulary

Primary students should continually expand their **perbendaharaan kata** (vocabulary) through reading and practice. This includes learning:

- **Synonyms (kata seerti)** and **antonyms (kata berlawan)** for common words
- Example: *besar* (big) vs *kecil* (small) are antonyms
- Example: *cantik* (pretty) has synonyms like *indah* or *jelita*

Building a strong vocabulary will aid in comprehension and make writing more interesting.

### Common Idioms and Proverbs (Peribahasa)

Malay has many idiomatic expressions and proverbs that primary students are expected to know. Peribahasa questions comprise about 5% of the PSLE Malay paper, so mastering them is important.

#### Essential Simpulan Bahasa (Two-Word Idioms)

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **ambil berat** | to take seriously, show care | Ibu ambil berat tentang pendidikan kami. |
| **anak angkat** | adopted child | Dia adalah anak angkat keluarga itu. |
| **anak emas** | favorite person (golden child) | Adik adalah anak emas ayah. |
| **buah tangan** | gift/souvenir | Saya membawa buah tangan untuk nenek. |
| **buruk siku** | taking back what was given | Jangan jadi orang yang buruk siku. |
| **cakar ayam** | messy handwriting | Tulisannya seperti cakar ayam. |
| **campur tangan** | to interfere | Jangan campur tangan dalam hal ini. |
| **hidung tinggi** | snobbish, arrogant | Dia bersikap hidung tinggi dengan kawan. |
| **kaki ayam** | barefoot | Budak itu berjalan kaki ayam. |
| **kaki bangku** | clumsy at sports | Saya kaki bangku dalam permainan bola. |
| **kecil hati** | hurt, offended | Dia berasa kecil hati dengan kata-kata itu. |
| **keras kepala** | stubborn | Adik sangat keras kepala. |
| **ringan tulang** | diligent, hard-working | Nenek saya ringan tulang membantu jiran. |
| **lapang dada** | broad-minded, patient | Kita harus lapang dada menerima kritikan. |
| **makan suap** | accept a bribe | Pegawai itu ditangkap kerana makan suap. |

#### Famous Malay Proverbs

**Bagai aur dengan tebing**
- **Meaning:** Close cooperation between two parties (like bamboo and riverbank)
- **Example:** Guru dan pelajar bekerjasama bagai aur dengan tebing.

**Air dicencang tiada putus**
- **Meaning:** Sibling bonds cannot be severed by quarrels
- **Example:** Walaupun bergaduh, mereka tetap bersatu kerana air dicencang tiada putus.

**Seperti katak di bawah tempurung**
- **Meaning:** Person with limited knowledge
- **Example:** Jangan jadi seperti katak di bawah tempurung, banyakkanlah membaca.

**Berat sama dipikul, ringan sama dijinjing**
- **Meaning:** Share burdens together (teamwork)
- **Example:** Dalam keluarga kami, berat sama dipikul, ringan sama dijinjing.

### Learning Strategy: Using Context

When learning new words or idioms, practice figuring out meaning from context. In comprehension passages, unfamiliar words can often be understood by reading surrounding sentences for clues.

**Practice Tips:**
- Create flashcards or a personal "word bank"
- Group new words by themes (weather, emotions, school)
- Include example sentences
- Read Malay stories, folktales, or news articles
- Make matching quizzes for peribahasa

## Grammar Fundamentals (Tatabahasa)

Mastering **tatabahasa** is crucial, as the language use section of exams heavily tests grammar. In PSLE Malay Paper 2, about 10% of the marks test choosing the correct grammar or word form in a passage.

### Parts of Speech (Golongan Kata)

#### 1. Kata Nama (Nouns)

Words for people, places, things, or ideas.

**Examples:** budak (child), sekolah (school), meja (table)

**Pronouns (Kata Ganti Nama):**
- **saya** - I (formal)
- **aku** - I (informal)
- **awak/kamu** - you
- **dia** - he/she
- **kami** - we (exclusive, excludes listener)
- **kita** - we (inclusive, includes listener)
- **mereka** - they

#### 2. Kata Kerja (Verbs)

Action or state words. Malay verbs are NOT inflected for tense.

**Examples:** makan (eat), tidur (sleep), bermain (play)

**Time Indicators:**
- **sedang** - ongoing (is doing)
- **telah** - completed (has done)
- **akan** - future (will do)
- **semalam** - yesterday
- **esok** - tomorrow

**Example:**
- Saya **sedang** makan. (I am eating.)
- Dia **telah** tidur. (He/she has slept.)
- Mereka **akan** bermain. (They will play.)

#### 3. Kata Adjektif (Adjectives)

Words that describe nouns. Malay adjectives come **after** the noun.

**Examples:**
- budak **pandai** (clever child)
- cuaca **panas** (hot weather)
- baju **merah** (red shirt)

**Forming Comparisons:**
- **lebih … daripada** - more … than
- **paling …** - most …
- **seperti** - like/as (for similes)

**Examples:**
- Ali **lebih tinggi daripada** Siti. (Ali is taller than Siti.)
- Ini adalah buku yang **paling menarik**. (This is the most interesting book.)
- Putih **seperti** kapas. (As white as cotton.)

#### 4. Function Words (Kata Tugas)

##### Kata Sendi Nama (Prepositions)

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **di** | at/in/on | di sekolah (at school) |
| **ke** | to/towards | ke rumah (to home) |
| **dari** | from | dari pasar (from market) |
| **daripada** | from/than | lebih baik daripada (better than) |
| **dengan** | with | dengan kawan (with friend) |
| **untuk** | for | untuk awak (for you) |
| **kepada** | to (person) | kepada ibu (to mother) |
| **antara** | among/between | antara kita (among us) |

**Important:** *di* and *ke* as prepositions are always written **separately** from the noun.

##### Kata Hubung (Conjunctions)

- **dan** - and
- **atau** - or
- **tetapi** - but
- **kerana** - because
- **jika** - if
- **walaupun** - although
- **supaya** - so that

**Example:** Ali dan Siti pergi ke kedai **kerana** mereka perlu membeli buku.

##### Kata Tanya (Question Words)

- **apa** - what
- **siapa** - who
- **bila** - when
- **di mana** - where
- **mengapa** - why
- **bagaimana** - how

**Example:** **Apa** hobi kamu? (What is your hobby?)

### Sentence Structure (Susunan Ayat)

Basic Malay sentence: **Subject – Predicate**

**Examples:**
- Bapa saya seorang guru. (My father [is] a teacher.)
- Adik makan nasi. (Little brother eats rice.)
- Cuaca hari ini panas. (Today's weather [is] hot.)

**Note:** Malay doesn't use "is/are" in equational sentences.

### Types of Sentences

1. **Ayat Penyata (Statements)**
   - Cuaca hari ini panas. (Today's weather is hot.)

2. **Ayat Tanya (Questions)**
   - Adakah kamu sudah siap? (Are you ready?)
   - Di manakah buku saya? (Where is my book?)

3. **Ayat Perintah (Commands)**
   - Tutup pintu itu. (Close that door.)
   - Sila duduk. (Please sit.)

4. **Ayat Seruan (Exclamations)**
   - Alangkah indahnya pemandangan ini! (How beautiful this view is!)

### Punctuation (Tanda Baca)

- **.** (tanda noktah) - period for statements
- **?** (tanda soal) - question mark
- **!** (tanda seru) - exclamation mark
- **,** (tanda koma) - comma
- **"..."** (tanda pengikat kata) - quotation marks

**Example:** Ali berkata, "Saya akan pergi sekarang."

### Active vs Passive Voice

**Active (Ayat Aktif):**
- Ibu **membaca** buku itu. (Mother reads the book.)

**Passive (Ayat Pasif):**
- Buku itu **dibaca** oleh ibu. (The book is read by Mother.)

**Important Distinction:**
- **di** (separate) = preposition meaning "at/in"
- **di-** (attached) = passive prefix

### Numerical Classifiers (Penjodoh Bilangan)

Malay uses specific classifiers when numbering nouns.

| Classifier | Used For | Example |
|------------|----------|---------|
| **orang** | people | seorang guru (one teacher) |
| **ekor** | animals | seekor kucing (one cat) |
| **buah** | large objects/abstract | sebuah rumah (a house) |
| **biji** | small, round objects | lima biji epal (five apples) |
| **batang** | long, stick-like objects | sebatang pensel (one pencil) |
| **helai** | thin, flat items | sehelai kertas (a sheet of paper) |
| **pasang** | pairs | sepasang kasut (a pair of shoes) |
| **keping** | flat, thin pieces | sekeping roti (a slice of bread) |

**Example:** Saya ada tiga **ekor** ikan. (I have three fish.)

## Affixes and Word Formation (Imbuhan)

One of the most tested components in Primary Malay is **imbuhan**, or affixation. In PSLE Paper 2, the Imbuhan section alone carries 10 questions (worth 20 marks, ~10% of the paper).

### Understanding Affixes

Malay is an agglutinative language - it sticks affixes onto base words to create new words or indicate grammatical relationships.

**Example:** Root word **masak** (to cook)
- **memasak** - cooks/is cooking (active verb)
- **memasakkan** - cooks for someone (causative)
- **dimasak** - is cooked (passive)
- **pemasak** - a cook (noun)
- **masakan** - cuisine/dish (noun)

### Major Prefixes (Awalan)

#### 1. meN- Prefix (Active Verbs)

Forms active verbs. Has several variations based on the first letter of the root word:

| Form | Used Before | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **me-** | l, m, n, r, w, y, vowels | melihat (to see), menarik (to pull) |
| **mem-** | b, f, p, v (p drops) | membaca (to read), memukul (to hit) |
| **men-** | c, j, d, t, s (t/s drop) | mencari (to search), menulis (to write) |
| **meng-** | vowels, g, h, k, kh (k drops) | mengira (to count), mengaji (to recite) |
| **meny-** | s (s drops) | menyapu (to sweep) |

**Examples:**
- sapu → **menyapu** (sweeps)
- tulis → **menulis** (writes)
- baca → **membaca** (reads)
- lihat → **melihat** (sees)

#### 2. di- Prefix (Passive Voice)

Indicates passive voice. No assimilation occurs.

**Examples:**
- **dimakan** (is eaten)
- **ditulis** (is written)
- **ditangkap** (is caught)

**Example Sentence:** Surat itu **ditulis** oleh Amin. (The letter was written by Amin.)

#### 3. ber- Prefix

Means "to have" or "to do." Often makes intransitive verbs.

**Examples:**
- **bermain** (to play)
- **berlari** (to run)
- **berbaju** merah (wearing red clothes)
- **beradik** tiga (have 3 siblings)
- **belajar** (to study) - irregular form

#### 4. ter- Prefix

Has multiple uses:
- **Accidental/involuntary actions:** tertumpah (accidentally spilled)
- **Superlative:** terbaik (the best)
- **Ability/passive:** terlihat (visible, able to be seen)

**Examples:**
- Saya **tertidur**. (I dozed off unintentionally.)
- Ini adalah buku **terbaik**. (This is the best book.)
- Pintu itu **terbuka**. (The door is open.)

#### 5. peN- Prefix (Nouns)

Forms nouns indicating a person or thing that performs an action. Follows same assimilation rules as meN-.

**Examples:**
- ajar → **pengajar** (teacher)
- tulis → **penulis** (writer)
- baca → **pembaca** (reader)
- sapu → **penyapu** (broom)

### Suffixes (Akhiran)

#### -kan Suffix

Turns verbs into transitive/causative verbs or marks imperatives.

**Examples:**
- letak → **letakkan** (put [something])
- kenal → **perkenalkan** (introduce)
- duduk → **dudukkan** (seat someone)

**Command:** Silakan duduk. (Please have a seat.)

#### -i Suffix

Indicates acting on something, often with repeated or focused action.

**Examples:**
- hormat → **hormati** (to respect)
- isi → **isikan** (to fill)

#### -an Suffix

Forms nouns from verbs or adjectives.

**Examples:**
- lukis → **lukisan** (a drawing)
- masak → **masakan** (cooked food, cuisine)
- panas → **kepanasan** (heat)

### Circumfixes (Apitan)

Combination of prefix and suffix used together.

#### pe-...-an

Forms abstract nouns or action nouns.

**Examples:**
- didik → **pendidikan** (education)
- bersih → **pembersihan** (cleaning)
- jaga → **penjagaan** (care)

#### ke-...-an

Forms nouns indicating state or condition.

**Examples:**
- putus → **keputusan** (decision)
- ada → **keadaan** (situation)
- sihat → **kesihatan** (health)

### Reduplication (Kata Ganda)

Repeating words to indicate plural, intensity, or variety.

**Examples:**
- rumah-rumah (houses)
- besar-besar (truly big ones)
- orang-orang (people)
- berjalan-jalan (to stroll)
- kanak-kanak (children)
- sayur-mayur (all kinds of vegetables)

## Reading Comprehension Skills (Kemahiran Kefahaman)

Reading comprehension (kefahaman membaca) is critical. By Primary 6, students face both multiple-choice and open-ended comprehension questions based on various text types.

### Strategies for Success

#### 1. Skimming and Scanning

- **Skim** the passage first for general idea
- Read the **questions** carefully
- **Scan** for keywords from questions in the text

#### 2. Understanding Question Types

**Literal/Factual Questions:**
- Answer is directly stated in text
- Look for specific information

**Inferential Questions:**
- Require deduction based on hints
- Read between the lines

**Question Words:**
- **Mengapakah** - Why (look for cause/reason)
- **Apakah kesan** - What is the effect
- **Apakah maksud** - What is the meaning
- **Pada pendapat kamu** - In your opinion

#### 3. Answering in Malay

**Always answer in complete sentences** unless instructed otherwise.

**Example:**
- Question: Mengapakah Amir pergi ke rumah kawannya?
- Good Answer: Amir pergi ke rumah kawannya **kerana** dia ingin belajar bersama-sama.
- Poor Answer: Untuk belajar. (incomplete sentence)

#### 4. Using Context Clues

If you encounter unfamiliar words:
- Read surrounding sentences
- Look at the tone
- Infer meaning from context

#### 5. Time Management

- Don't spend too long on one question
- Mark difficult questions and return later
- Kefahaman 1 (MCQ) - shorter passage
- Kefahaman 2 (Mixed) - two passages with MCQ and open-ended

### Practice Tips

- Use past year papers
- Check answers and learn from mistakes
- Note vocabulary that tripped you up
- Read various Malay materials regularly
- Practice identifying main ideas

## Oral Communication and Listening Skills (Lisan & Mendengar)

The oral component carries 25% of PSLE marks. Students must read aloud fluently and engage in conversation.

### Reading Aloud (Membaca Lantang)

#### Pronunciation (Sebutan)

**Malay has phonetic spelling** - pronounce every syllable.

**Two "e" sounds:**
- **e taling** (clear): as in **e**kor
- **e pepet** (schwa): as in **e**mas, b**e**rharap

**The Malay 'r':**
- Typically trilled or rolled slightly
- Practice: kereta, harimau, perang

#### Intonation and Pacing

- Read with natural rise and fall
- Questions should have questioning tone at end
- Use pauses at commas and periods
- Don't read in monotone

**Example with pauses:**
"Tanpa membuang masa, Ali terus berlari pulang ke rumahnya, **[pause]** lalu memberitahu ibunya tentang kejadian itu."

#### Expression (Ekspresi)

- Convey the mood of the passage
- Happy occasions = upbeat voice
- Sad/serious = somber tone
- Dialogues = slightly vary tone for characters

**Example:**
""Tolong! Tolong!" jerit budak itu dengan cemas."
- Say "Tolong! Tolong!" with panic
- Return to narrative tone for the rest

#### Tips for Success

1. **Use preparation time wisely** (10 minutes given)
2. **Practice difficult words** beforehand
3. **Plan where to pause** for breath
4. **Speak loud and clear** - not shouting, but audible
5. **Avoid fillers** like "err..." or English words
6. **Don't rush** - articulate each word

### Conversation (Perbualan)

#### Understanding the Stimulus

You may be shown a video clip or pictures, then asked to discuss themes like:
- Kindness
- Healthy living
- Environment
- School activities
- Community values

#### Structure Your Response

**Three-Part Answer:**
1. **State your main point** upfront
2. **Give reasons or examples** (1-2)
3. **Conclude** with personal feeling

**Example:**
**Question:** Mengapakah penting untuk menjaga alam sekitar?

**Answer:**
"Pada pendapat saya, menjaga alam sekitar amat penting **kerana** kesihatan semua makhluk bergantung padanya. **Sebagai contoh**, jika kita tidak membuang sampah dengan betul, sungai dan laut akan tercemar. **Selain itu**, perubahan iklim boleh berlaku jika kita menebang pokok sewenang-wenangnya. **Oleh itu**, setiap orang harus memainkan peranan untuk memelihara alam sekitar."

**Note the use of connectors:** kerana, sebagai contoh, selain itu, oleh itu

#### Useful Connectors for Conversation

- **kerana** - because
- **sebagai contoh** - for example
- **selain itu** - besides that
- **oleh itu** - therefore
- **pada pendapat saya** - in my opinion
- **saya percaya** - I believe

#### Conversation Tips

1. **Use appropriate vocabulary** for the theme
2. **Include peribahasa** if relevant and natural
3. **Speak fluently** - perfection not required
4. **If you blank, rephrase** rather than stay silent
5. **Maintain eye contact** with examiner
6. **Ask for clarification** if needed: "Maaf Cikgu, boleh ulangi?"
7. **Use formal Malay** - say "saya" not "aku"
8. **Avoid rojak** (mixing languages)

### Listening Comprehension (Kefahaman Mendengar)

Students listen to recorded passages and answer MCQ questions.

#### Success Strategies

1. **Train your ear** - listen to Malay regularly
   - TV programs
   - Radio (Warna 94.2FM)
   - YouTube content
   - Language apps

2. **During the exam:**
   - Audio plays **twice**
   - First listen: focus on understanding
   - Second listen: confirm answers
   - Read questions **before** audio plays

3. **Take quick notes** - jot keywords only
4. **Watch for trap answers** - options may sound similar

## Writing Skills and Composition (Penulisan Karangan)

By Primary 6, students write a **karangan** (composition) of at least 120-150 words in 50 minutes. Usually choice between open-ended topic or picture-guided.

### Composition Structure

#### 1. Pendahuluan (Introduction)

Set the scene or context.

**Examples:**
- Pada suatu pagi yang hening, ... (On a quiet morning...)
- Malang tidak berbau. (Misfortune comes unannounced.)

#### 2. Perkembangan/Isi (Body)

2-3 paragraphs detailing events chronologically.

**Use transitional phrases:**
- Pertama (Firstly)
- Kemudian (Then)
- Seterusnya (Next)
- Tiba-tiba (Suddenly)
- Selepas itu (After that)
- Akhirnya (Finally)

#### 3. Penutup (Conclusion)

Wrap up and reflect on lesson learned.

**Examples:**
- Peristiwa itu akan sentiasa terpahat dalam ingatan saya.
- Saya belajar bahawa kita mestilah berhati-hati.

### Responding to Picture Prompts

If given 3-4 pictures:
- Write at least one paragraph per picture
- Weave into a logical narrative
- Add details beyond literal description
- Connect pictures smoothly

### Language and Style Tips

#### Use Varied Vocabulary

**Instead of repeating "gembira":**
- suka hati
- riang
- teruja

**Instead of repeating "marah":**
- berang
- murka
- geram

#### Descriptive Words

- **termangu-mangu** (stunned/confused)
- **merenung** (to gaze)
- **tersenyum** (smiled)
- **menangis teresak-esak** (sobbing)

#### Include Idioms Naturally

"Kami bergotong-royong membersihkan taman – **berat sama dipikul, ringan sama dijinjing** – kerja berat menjadi mudah apabila dilakukan bersama."

#### Check Grammar and Spelling

- Use correct imbuhan for verbs
- Ensure di- vs di spacing is correct
- Check verb forms (don't add unnecessary prefixes)
- Use proper punctuation

### Time Management

- **5-10 minutes:** Planning and outline
- **35-40 minutes:** Writing
- **5 minutes:** Proofreading

**During proofreading check:**
- Missing or incorrect imbuhan
- Spelling errors
- Sentence clarity
- Consistent perspective (first person vs third person)

### Writing Practice Tips

1. **Keep a Malay journal** - write daily
2. **Read model compositions** - study high-scoring examples
3. **Get feedback** - from teachers or proficient speakers
4. **Practice various topics** - build flexibility
5. **Prepare useful phrases** - toolkit of openings, conclusions, proverbs
6. **Write legibly** - paragraph clearly with indentation

## Self-Study Tips and Continued Learning

### Immerse Yourself Daily

- **Watch:** Malay TV shows, YouTube channels
- **Listen:** Malay songs, radio (Warna 94.2FM)
- **Read:** Storybooks, comics, newspapers (Berita Harian)
- **Speak:** Practice with family, friends, language buddies

### Use Technology

- **MEKAR e-learning portal** (MOE-endorsed)
- **Quizlet** for flashcard sets
- **Language apps** (Talkpal, etc.) for speaking practice
- **AI chatbots** for conversation practice

### Active Practice

- **Label items at home** in Malay (sticky notes)
- **Speak only Malay** for set periods daily
- **Summarize** shows/books in Malay
- **Create flashcards** for new vocabulary
- **Join language clubs** or community activities

### Set Personal Challenges

- Learn 5 new idioms per week
- Speak Malay-only hour daily
- Write 3 journal entries weekly
- Complete 2 comprehension exercises weekly

### Stay Motivated

- **Celebrate small wins** - using Malay successfully in real life
- **Track progress** - notice improvements in tests
- **Remember your why** - bilingualism is an asset
- **Enjoy the journey** - engage with Malay media and culture
